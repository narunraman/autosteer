Benchmarks

We use benchmark to mean a specification of what is measured and how: a taxonomy of elements, a generator that produces questions for each element from templates, and the rules for scoring answers. A dataset is a fixed sample of questions drawn from a benchmark. The datasets we publish are for results that need to be comparable across papers; the generator, available through the API and autoSTEER, is for fresh questions.

Two angles on economic reasoning

We provide two benchmarks, each taking a distinct angle on economic decision-making.

In strategic settings the best choice depends on how other agents will respond: a phone manufacturer setting its quantity and price has to anticipate its competitors. This is a well-defined and rich area, but a small part of economics as a field.

In non-strategic settings each agent is too small to move the market and takes prices as given: a small farm choosing between wheat and corn at today's prices is not playing against anyone. These settings make up much of microeconomics: consumers, firms, competitive markets and welfare.

The benchmarks are described in the following articles:

BenchmarkDescription
STEERStrategic and behavioral decision-making. It breaks decision theory and game theory into elements of economic rationality: the foundations, choosing alone under certainty and risk, choosing against other agents in games, and choosing on behalf of a group.
STEER-MENon-strategic microeconomics. It tests whether a model can reason as a price-taking consumer or firm, add individual choices up into markets, find the prices that clear them, and judge whether the outcome is efficient.

Both are built the same way: elements grouped into modules and settings, questions that vary in type, domain and perspective, and the same scoring. STEER-ME starts from the same foundations as STEER, so the elements they have in common are published once, in STEER.

Key terms

Element
One ability, such as applying the law of demand.
Template
A question with labeled fields where the numbers go.
Question
A template filled in, with options and a correct answer computed by code.
Type
The form a question takes within an element.
Domain
Its subject matter, such as finance or medicine.
Perspective
The point of view it is written from.
